01 · Principles

Editorial, warm, exact.

The site should feel like a well-made printed portfolio that happens to move. Five rules decide most questions.

  1. Light canvas, dark bandsEvery page sits on warm off-white. Dark appears only as a full-width band inside a page.
  2. Square and groundedZero radius everywhere. Depth comes from hard offset shadows, not blur.
  3. Two typefaces, clear rolesDM Serif Display speaks. Plus Jakarta Sans explains and labels.
  4. Blue means actionAccent is for CTAs, active states and one emphasis word per heading.
  5. Motion confirms, never performsShort, springy feedback on hover and reveal. Everything still works with motion off.
  6. Real numbers onlyStats and claims come from the work. No invented metrics, no placeholder praise.

02 · Light & dark

Dark is a band, never a page.

The old case studies ran fully dark while the homepage was light, so moving between them felt like changing sites. From now on every page uses the light canvas. Dark bands are allowed in three places only, and they keep the same type and spacing as light sections.

07 · Motion

Short and springy.

Hover a card to play its curve. Always list the properties you transition (never all), stagger reveals by 80ms with style="--i:n", and respect reduced motion. That is already handled in ds.css.

--ease-spring · reveals, arrows, badges
--ease-out · hovers, colour, images
--ease-curtain · fills, wipes

Durations

--dur-fast 150ms colour · --dur-base 220ms hover lift · --dur-slow 400ms fills · --dur-page 600ms reveals

Behaviours (site.js, opt-in by attribute)

.rv reveal · data-count count-up · data-magnetic · data-copy · data-clock · .rise headline lines

Buttons · .btn + .btn-primary / -secondary / -accent / -inv / -ghost · sizes .btn-sm / .btn-lg / .btn-block

09 · Case study pattern

Every case study tells the story in the same order.

Copy /work/eyeontask/index.html to /work/<slug>/index.html and swap the content. Keep the section order so readers always know where they are. See it live: EyeOnTask case study →

  1. Header · .cs-hero: crumbs, tags, .t-h1 with one <em> word, a lead summary, and a .cs-meta list (role, duration, platform, tools)
  2. Cover · .cs-cover: the strongest screen, with the xl shadow
  3. Impact · .stat-grid: 3–4 real numbers, before→after where you have them
  4. Overview · .cs-section + .prose: the context in two short paragraphs
  5. Problem · .figure--split + .pull: what was broken, shown and stated
  6. Solution · .figure + one .ps card per decision
  7. Outcomes · .outcomes: what shipped, and what changed
  8. Next case · .next-case on .band-alt
  9. Footer · .footer: the same contact block as the homepage

Before publishing a new page

  • Links only ds.css and site.js. No page-level <style> unless the layout is truly one-off (like the homepage hero).
  • Light canvas. Dark only as .band-dark, .strip or .footer.
  • Exactly one <h1>. Every heading uses a role class.
  • Every image has alt text, a border, and sits in a .figure, .cs-cover or card.
  • Every number is real and comes from the project.
  • Checked at 375px wide with no sideways scroll.

10 · Do & don't

Quick rules.

Do

  • Use tokens for every colour, space and shadow
  • Keep body copy at weight 400 and under 68ch wide
  • Give images a 1px border and 0 radius
  • Use one blue emphasis word per heading
  • Lift on hover: translate(-2px,-2px) plus a bigger shadow

Don't

  • Build a fully dark page
  • Round any corner or use blurred shadows
  • Use Inter, Roboto or system fonts
  • Fill a full section with the accent blue
  • Write transition: all or add one-off pixel values
