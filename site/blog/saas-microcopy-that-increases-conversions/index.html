SaaS Microcopy That Increases Conversions: Button Text, Password Hints and Error Messages That Remove User Hesitation

If any button in your product says 'Submit', this post is for you.

Durga PrasadSenior Product Designer · 8+ years in fintech & B2B SaaS

Quick answer

Microcopy is the small text in an interface: button labels, hints under fields and error messages. Good SaaS microcopy tells people what happens when they click, shows the rules before they make a mistake and explains errors in plain words with a next step. These small changes reduce hesitation and help more people finish forms.

Key takeaways

  • Button labels should say what the user gets, not 'Submit'.
  • Show password rules before people type, and tick them off live.
  • Error messages should say what went wrong and what to do next.
  • Your support tickets are a list of microcopy bugs.

If any button in your product says "Submit", change it today.

Nobody wakes up excited to submit something. It sounds like tax paperwork.

That's microcopy: the button labels, the hint text under fields and the messages that appear when something goes wrong. Most teams write it at the last minute, or keep whatever an engineer typed during the build.

In my experience, fixing these tiny phrases often does more than a full visual redesign.

Why? Because words are the voice of your interface. Users don't read paragraphs. They read buttons.

What is microcopy in SaaS?

Microcopy is any short piece of text that helps someone take the next step: "Create my free account", "We'll never share your email", "That card number looks incomplete".

It's small. It's also the last thing people read before they decide to click or leave.

Which microcopy changes lift SaaS conversions?

1. Replace "Submit" with the outcome

Tell people what they get:

  • "Submit" → "Create my free account"
  • "Proceed" → "Go to dashboard"
  • "Download" → "Get the free checklist"

Remind them what's waiting on the other side of the click.

2. Show password rules before people type

You enter your usual password, click create, and only then get the red text: "Must contain 12 characters, 1 uppercase letter, 1 number and 1 ancient hieroglyph."

Infuriating.

Show the rules up front, and tick them off live as each one is met. Don't punish people for rules you never told them.

3. Write errors like a helpful person

Not this: "Error: Card validation failed."

This: "We couldn't verify this card with your bank. Check the expiry date or try another card."

Say what went wrong and what to do next. Calm people down. Guide them back.

How do you find the microcopy worth fixing?

  • Search your codebase or Figma for "Submit", "Click here", "Invalid" and "Error".
  • Read your support tickets. Every "what does this mean?" is a microcopy bug.
  • Watch someone fill in your signup form without helping them. Note every pause.

The takeaway

Every word in your interface either builds confidence or creates doubt.

Give your microcopy the same care you give your backend.

FAQ

What is microcopy in UX?

Microcopy is the short text that guides people through an interface: button labels, field hints, confirmation messages and error messages.

What should a call-to-action button say?

What the user gets by clicking, in their words. 'Start my free trial' or 'Get the checklist' works better than 'Submit' or 'Click here'.

How do you write a good error message?

Say what happened in plain words, don't blame the user, and give a clear next step. For payments, also say whether any money was taken.
