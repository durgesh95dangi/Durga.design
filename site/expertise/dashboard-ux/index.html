Context

Why it matters.

Dashboards shouldn't be vanity metric graveyards. They exist to trigger informed decisions. I architect enterprise dashboards with visual hierarchy, scannable summary cards, customizable data tables, and intuitive drill-down paths that allow executives and operators to spot anomalies in seconds.

Bottlenecks

Friction I solve.

  • Preventing cognitive paralysis caused by displaying 40 charts simultaneously
  • Designing high-density data tables with bulk actions, sorting, pin columns, and cell editing
  • Structuring progressive query filters that adapt to complex boolean parameters
  • Ensuring charts are fully legible across color-blind palettes and diverse screen resolutions

Principles

Rules I design by.

  1. Summary first, zoom and filter, then details-on-demand (Shneiderman's Mantra).
  2. Every visualization must answer an actionable question: 'So what should I do now?'
  3. Tables are software: pagination, sticky headers, and keyboard row traversal are mandatory.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How do you decide what belongs on the main dashboard view?

We conduct operator interviews to identify the top 3 decisions made daily. Only metrics directly feeding those 3 decisions make the primary viewport; everything else is accessible via drill-downs.

What makes an enterprise table usable?

Configurable columns, persistent filters, sticky header/row IDs, batch actions, and high cell contrast without decorative padding bloat.
