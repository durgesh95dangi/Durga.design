## Article

*'Our users only use desktop.' Maybe. Your buyers don't.*

**Quick answer:** Mobile UX matters for B2B SaaS because decision-makers often discover and judge products on their phones, through LinkedIn, email or a colleague's link. A broken mobile page makes the product look careless. You don't need the full app on mobile, but you need a fast landing page, a way to continue on desktop and simple views for quick checks.

**Key takeaways**
- Your users and your buyers are different people with different devices.
- A broken mobile page makes the whole product look careless.
- You need a solid mobile landing page and a 'continue on desktop' option, not the full app.
- Test on a real iPhone and a real Android phone. They break differently.

"We don't care about mobile right now. Our product is a complex desktop tool. Our users sit at dual monitors."

I hear this from B2B founders all the time.

It sounds logical. But it mixes up two different people: the user and the buyer.

## Does mobile UX matter for B2B SaaS?

Yes, because of how buying decisions actually start.

Picture your ideal buyer: a VP of Operations, a CFO, a Head of Engineering. At 2 PM they're in back-to-back meetings. They're not researching tools.

They find you at 7:30 AM, scrolling LinkedIn with their coffee. Or on the train home. Or on a Saturday, when a colleague drops your link in Slack.

They tap. They land on your site on a phone. And they see:

- A popup that overflows the screen, with the close button off-screen.
- A pricing table that breaks the page width.
- A menu that won't open in mobile Safari.

Do they bookmark you for Monday? No. They assume your engineering is sloppy and move to the next tab.

![B2B SaaS website broken on mobile with an off-screen popup](https://durga.design/assets/media/images/blog/inline/mobile-ux-for-b2b-saas-hidden-traffic-1.webp)

## What mobile experience does a B2B SaaS product actually need?

You don't need your full data-modelling engine on a 375px screen. You need three things:

1. **A mobile landing page that works perfectly.** Fast, readable, with screenshots or a short video that fit the screen.
2. **A "continue on desktop" handshake.** If the app really needs a keyboard, let mobile visitors enter their email once and get a magic link: "Email me a link to open on my laptop."
3. **Simple views for quick checks.** Even in heavy tools, people want to approve an invoice, check today's numbers or see an alert from their phone.

![Mobile screen offering a magic link to continue on desktop](https://durga.design/assets/media/images/blog/inline/mobile-ux-for-b2b-saas-hidden-traffic-2.webp)

## How do you test your B2B site on mobile?

Quick fix, 15 minutes:

- Open your homepage, pricing page and signup on a real phone, not a resized browser.
- Try to close every popup and open every menu.
- Sign up on the phone. Note every place you get stuck.

Do it on iPhone Safari and on an Android phone with Chrome. They break differently.

## The takeaway

Mobile isn't where your users do their heavy work.

It's where your buyers decide if you're worth a desktop visit.

Your mobile landing page has the same job as your desktop one. Here's [the anatomy of a high-converting SaaS landing page](https://durga.design/blog/anatomy-of-high-converting-saas-landing-page/).

## FAQ

**Do B2B SaaS products need a mobile app?**
Not always. Many need a responsive marketing site and a few mobile-friendly views for quick tasks more than a full native app. Look at what your users and buyers actually try to do on their phones.

**How do I make a complex SaaS table work on mobile?**
Show only the most important columns, turn rows into cards, or let people scroll sideways with the first column fixed. For heavy analysis, offer a way to continue on desktop.

**How do I know how many B2B visitors use mobile?**
Check the device report in your analytics for your marketing pages and your signup flow separately. They're often very different from what the product itself sees.
